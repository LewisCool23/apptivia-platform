Section 02

The Problem

Sales teams are drowning in tools but starving for insight. The average B2B sales organization uses 10+ tools, yet managers still coach by gut and reps still waste the majority of their time on non-revenue activities.

Three Failure Modes

Failure Mode 1: Invisible Performance
Reps don't know where they stand until it's too late. Quarterly reviews surface problems 90 days after they start. Weekly KPIs — when they exist — live in spreadsheets that nobody opens. By the time a manager sees the trend, the rep is already on a PIP or has quit.

Failure Mode 2: Gut-Based Coaching
Managers coach based on intuition, not data. They spend their limited coaching time on whoever is loudest, not whoever needs it most. High performers get ignored; struggling reps get generic advice. The $15B spent on sales training annually has no feedback loop to measure impact.

Failure Mode 3: Cold Outreach in a Warm World
Reps blast generic messages to cold lists while intent signals go unnoticed. Companies showing buying behavior — hiring, funding, competitor engagement — aren't prioritized because the data is scattered across 5+ tools that don't talk to each other.

How It Works (5-Step Flywheel)

  1. Connect (10 min): Org admin configures 5 key KPIs via guided onboarding. Apptivia ingests data from CRM, phone system, and calendar via integration framework (10 connectors built: Salesforce, HubSpot, Outreach, Gong, SalesLoft, Marketo, Apollo, Google Calendar, Microsoft Calendar, Sendoso).

  5. Prospect (Signal-Driven): Engage surfaces companies showing buying signals from 45+ signal types. AI drafts personalized outreach and queues it for human approval. Pipeline Operator tracks deals through configurable CEP stages with AI forecast notes.

Multi-Tenant Security

Org-Scoping Audit Complete (April 2026): Every INSERT operation across all 20 pages and 98 components has been verified for organization_id inclusion. RLS policies enforce row-level isolation. Tables without their own org_id column (contest_participants, profile_badges, profile_achievements) use FK-based RLS joins through parent tables. 3 missing org_id inserts identified and fixed in the coaching plans flow.

  • Row-Level Security: Every table has RLS policies scoping queries to the authenticated user's organization_id
  • Profile-Based Isolation: All API endpoints run through loadProfile middleware that validates org membership and joins organization data (subscription plan, status, trial) in a single query
  • FK-Scoped Tables: Child tables (contest_participants, profile_badges, etc.) inherit org isolation through foreign key joins to org-scoped parent tables
  • Role-Based Access: 4 roles (Admin, Manager, Coach, Power User) with granular permission flags per feature
  • Leadership Exclusion: Admins and managers automatically excluded from scorecard/KPI views to prevent data contamination

Business Model Framework (Cornell Intermediary Model)

Apptivia functions as an expanded intermediary between raw data sources (CRM, dialer, email, calendar) and sales team decision-making:

  • Matching: Signal data routed to the right rep at the right time
  • Trust: Human-in-the-loop approval layer prevents AI spam
  • Logistics: 14 automated cron jobs handle scheduling, scoring, alerting, intelligence gathering
  • Value-Added Intelligence: Scoring, trend analysis, coaching, and prospecting on top of raw data

The platform disintermediates the sales ops analyst who spends 10 hours/week building reports manually, and creates a new market by connecting AI insights to human sales actions with an approval layer.

Future Pricing Evolution

As cross-org benchmark data matures and Apptivia accumulates coaching outcome data across multiple customer organizations, the platform will evaluate outcome-based pricing as a complementary tier. In this model, customers at scale would pay a performance premium tied to measurable improvements in quota attainment — directly aligning Apptivia's revenue to the customer outcomes it produces.

This evolution is enabled by Apptivia's unique position as the system of record for rep performance data. No competitor without equivalent data depth can offer this model credibly. It is not the current go-to-market motion — the current priority is proving per-seat subscription economics — but it represents a defensible long-term pricing lever unavailable to point-tool competitors.

This mirrors the servitization model: moving from selling platform access to selling measurable performance outcomes. It is revisited when cross-org benchmarks reach statistical significance (estimated: 50+ customer organizations).
